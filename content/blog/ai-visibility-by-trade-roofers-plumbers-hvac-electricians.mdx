Ask ChatGPT for a plumber in Fort Worth and it researches the question one way. Ask it for a roofer in the same zip code and it researches a different set of sources, weighs different credentials, and names companies for different reasons.

Most AI visibility advice treats "contractor" as one category. It isn't. The engines are the same, the [six signals](/research/the-6signal-audit-what-we-check) are the same, but the *weighting* changes by trade — because the buyer's question changes, and because each trade leaves a different paper trail on the public internet for AI to verify.

This post maps those differences for the four trades we get asked about most in Dallas–Fort Worth: plumbers, roofers, HVAC companies, and electricians.

*Where your trade lives on this line is not a choice. How you prepare for it is.*

At the emergency end, the buyer asks one question — often out loud, often on a phone — and takes the first credible answer. We covered why that makes [emergency trades the most exposed to AI search](/research/why-emergency-trades-are-most-exposed-to-ai-search) and why [voice answers make it worse](/research/voice-search-for-contractors-siri-alexa-google-assistant): a screen shows ten results; a spoken answer reads one.

At the considered end, buyers compare. That widens the field from one name to a handful — but if AI doesn't name you at all, you are not in the handful either. [Ranking is not the same as being recommended](/research/ranking-is-not-the-same-as-being-recommended) in every trade; urgency just decides how brutal the difference is.

<h2 id="plumbers">Plumbers: the measured baseline</h2>

Plumbing is the one trade where we can show you real numbers instead of reasoning, because our tracking system ran [700 automated checks of real buyer prompts in one DFW plumbing market](/research/we-ran-700-ai-visibility-checks-one-texas-market) between July 7 and August 3, 2026.

Two findings matter for every plumber reading this:

**The engines disagree wildly about the same company.** One well-run plumbing company was named in 92% of Gemini checks, 75% of ChatGPT checks, 71% of Perplexity checks — and 0% of Google AI Overview checks. Your visibility is not one number. It's a spread, and each engine has to be won separately.

**AI checks your paperwork.** Across the window, ChatGPT's research trail included the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners — tsbpe.texas.gov — 587 times. It consulted the state's own registry while deciding who to recommend. For a licensed trade, the boring institutional record *is* marketing now: if the name on your license, your [Google Business Profile](/research/how-to-fix-google-business-profile-for-ai-visibility), and your website don't match exactly, you're feeding the engine a reason to skip you.

<h2 id="roofers">Roofers: the manufacturer layer</h2>

Roofing in North Texas is event-driven — a hail line moves across Tarrant County and ten thousand homeowners start asking the same question in the same week. Two structural facts change the playbook:

**Certification directories are third-party proof AI can read.** GAF's contractor locator, Owens Corning's contractor network, CertainTeed's credentialed-installer listings — these are pages a manufacturer publishes about you, on a high-authority domain, with your name, city, and credential. That is exactly the class of independent, verifiable source AI engines lean on when [deciding whether to trust a business enough to name it](/research/the-source-ecosystem-third-party-mentions-ai-recommendations). A roofer with a maintained manufacturer listing has something a yard sign can't buy: an institution vouching for them in machine-readable public data.

**Storm surges reward whoever was visible before the storm.** AI engines synthesize from data that already exists. The roofer who shows up in AI answers the week after a hailstorm is the one whose entity record, reviews, and certifications were consistent the month before. We wrote the roofing-specific playbook in [AI Visibility for Roofers](/research/ai-visibility-for-roofers-new-local-seo); the short version is that preparation is the whole game.

<h2 id="hvac">HVAC: dealer networks and the Texas summer</h2>

HVAC splits across the whole urgency spectrum — a dead compressor in a Dallas August is an emergency; a system replacement in October is a considered purchase with three bids. That means HVAC companies need both ends covered: the [local entity layer](/research/local-entity-gap-ai-search) for the emergency query, and comparison-ready content for the replacement query, where buyers ask AI things like "is it worth replacing a 15-year-old AC unit in Texas."

HVAC also has the deepest dealer-program bench of any trade: Trane Comfort Specialist, Carrier Factory Authorized Dealer, Lennox dealer programs — Lennox International is headquartered in Richardson, in this market — Daikin Comfort Pro, Rheem Pro Partner. Every one of those is a manufacturer-published directory listing with your business name on it. Most DFW HVAC companies already hold at least one of these credentials and never think of it as visibility data. It is.

<h2 id="electricians">Electricians: the registry trade</h2>

Electrical work is the most credential-gated of the four — licensed through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, permitted through city portals, and bought with more fear of getting it wrong than any other trade. The buyer's question reflects it: "licensed electrician near me" carries the word *licensed* far more often than the plumbing equivalent.

That makes electricians the purest registry play. The businesses AI names confidently are the ones whose license record, GBP, website, and directory listings tell one consistent story — and whose [reviews say what AI needs to hear](/research/what-contractor-reviews-need-to-say-for-ai-visibility): the specific service, the specific city, the outcome. An electrician with 40 reviews that all say "great guy, on time" is invisible next to one whose reviews say "replaced our panel in Arlington, passed inspection first time."

<h2 id="seven-surfaces">The seven surfaces, ranked by trade</h2>

Every trade competes on the same seven surfaces. What changes is the order in which they pay. Ranked from most universal to most trade-dependent:

1. **Google Business Profile + Maps.** The foundation for all four trades, and the most common point of failure. The emergency trades feel it first — Maps is where "near me" gets answered — but no trade escapes it. [Fix it first](/research/how-to-fix-google-business-profile-for-ai-visibility).
2. **Your own website.** In our 700-check window, the tracked company's own site was the single most-consulted source across ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity — ahead of every directory. AI reads your site to verify what everyone else says about you. [If crawlers can't parse it](/research/is-your-site-blocking-ai-crawlers), everything downstream weakens.
3. **The big directories.** Angi, BBB, Yelp, HomeAdvisor, BuildZoom appeared in all three research engines' trails, hundreds of times each. You don't have to love them; [the engines keep citing them](/research/why-directories-and-aggregators-keep-winning-ai-answers). Claim, correct, and stop resenting.
4. **State license registries.** Heaviest for electricians and plumbers — TDLR and TSBPE are cross-referenced institutional truth. Exact-name consistency is the entire assignment.
5. **Manufacturer and dealer directories.** Heaviest for roofers and HVAC — certification listings are institutional endorsements published where AI can read them. Plumbers and electricians have fewer, but brand service-partner programs exist there too.
6. **Reviews with content.** All four trades, but the considered trades most — a remodel buyer asks AI to compare, and AI compares what reviewers actually wrote.
7. **Community mentions.** Reddit appeared over 2,300 times in ChatGPT's research trail for one plumbing market. You can't buy your way in — you earn it by being the name locals type when someone asks "who do y'all use?"

<h2 id="what-to-do">What to do with this</h2>

The order of operations doesn't change by trade — [LEO first, then IEO, then the answer layer](/research/the-6signal-visibility-audit-what-we-measure-and-why) — but the emphasis does:

- **Plumbers:** registry-name consistency and Maps. You're an emergency trade; the one answer read aloud has to be you.
- **Roofers:** claim every manufacturer credential you hold, then make your entity storm-ready before the season.
- **HVAC:** cover both ends — entity layer for July, comparison content for October, dealer listings always.
- **Electricians:** license-record consistency and reviews that name the job and the city.
