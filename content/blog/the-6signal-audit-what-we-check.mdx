## The six signals we check

The name 6Signal is not decoration.

Six distinct systems now decide whether a homeowner ever hears your company's name. The audit checks all six.

### 1. GEO — Generative Engine Optimization

When someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity for a contractor in your trade and city, your name is either in the answer or it isn't.

We check:

- Whether AI assistants name your company in recommendation answers
- Which competitors appear in your place
- What the machines actually say about your company when asked directly
- Whether third-party mentions and review specificity support a recommendation
- Whether your entity signals are strong enough for AI to trust you by name

### 2. AEO — Answer Engine Optimization

Google now answers questions directly, above the results, before anyone scrolls.

We check:

- Whether your company is cited in AI-generated answers for your trade and market
- Whether your pages answer the questions buyers actually ask
- FAQ and comparison content — the formats answer engines cite most
- Which sources own the answers in your market right now, and whether a directory is filling the space a local company should occupy

### 3. LEO — Local Entity Optimization

Every listing, citation, and review feeds the trust signals machines check before recommending anyone.

We check:

- Google Business Profile completeness — categories, services, service area, photos, hours
- Name, address, and phone consistency across Maps, Yelp, BBB, and the directories AI systems read
- Review volume, recency, and specificity — a review that names the service and the city is worth more than "great work"
- Whether the web agrees about who your company is

### 4. VEO — Voice Engine Optimization

A homeowner standing next to a burst pipe asks Siri who to call. Voice queries return one or two names, not ten.

We check:

- What Siri, Alexa, and Google Assistant return for your trade near your city
- Whether your Apple Maps presence is claimed and accurate — voice runs on the same local data
- Whether urgent, hands-free queries can resolve to your company

### 5. PEO — Prompt Engine Optimization

Buyers search with specific language: "emergency AC repair open now," "roofer for storm damage," "slab leak plumber near me."

We check:

- Whether your company surfaces inside the real query language your customers type and speak
- Which high-intent prompts trigger your name — and which skip you
- Whether the buyer's full question path is covered, from "what do I do when this breaks" to "who do I call"

### 6. IEO — Index Engine Optimization

Every AI and search system reads your site, schema, and business data to understand what you do and where you work.

We check:

- Whether AI crawlers can access and parse your site at all
- Schema markup — present, valid, and honest
- Site structure, headings, and service descriptions machines can actually extract
- Whether anything technical is quietly blocking the systems that would otherwise recommend you

## The order that usually wins

Not all six signals deserve attention first. In most contractor audits, leverage follows this order:

1. **LEO first.** Google Business Profile, reviews, and citation consistency move both Maps rankings and AI recommendations — the machines ground their local answers in exactly this data.
2. **IEO second.** Schema and crawlability are cheap, one-time fixes that everything else depends on.
3. **AEO third.** Question-answering service pages are what earn citations in AI answers.
4. **GEO and PEO compound.** Third-party presence and prompt coverage build over months, on top of the first three.
5. **VEO mostly inherits.** Clean local data is most of the voice battle.

Your audit ranks these for your specific situation — that ordering is the deliverable, not a generic checklist.

## Example findings

Here are common findings from contractor audits:

### Finding 1: AI recommends three competitors by name

The company does good work, but when a buyer asks ChatGPT or Gemini who to call, three other names come back — sometimes including a national aggregator instead of any local company.

Fix:

Strengthen the entity and review signals AI grounds on, and build the answer-ready content that earns citations.

### Finding 2: The service pages are too thin to cite

The company offers valuable services, but the pages have almost no useful content — nothing an answer engine can quote.

Fix:

Build deeper service pages with FAQs, process, proof, location relevance, and the specific questions buyers ask before calling.

### Finding 3: Reviews are strong but say nothing specific

The company has great reviews, but they read "great service" — no trade, no city, no outcome. Machines can't use them.

Fix:

A review strategy that produces specific language, plus surfacing the best reviews on the pages that need them.

### Finding 4: The directories disagree

Three different phone numbers across Yelp, HomeAdvisor, and Google. Every system that cross-references them loses confidence.

Fix:

Full entity cleanup — one name, one number, one story, everywhere.

### Finding 5: The site is invisible to machines

No schema, vague headings, service descriptions that never name the service area. AI systems describe the company in generic terms — or not at all.

Fix:

Structured data, machine-readable service pages, and clean business data across the site.
