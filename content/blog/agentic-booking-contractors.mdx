In May 2026, at its own developer conference, Google said its AI would start contacting local businesses on a user's behalf to book appointments. The first categories named were home repair, beauty, and pet care, with a US rollout planned for that summer.

Home repair was first on the list. That is a sentence worth sitting with if you own a trades business.

What follows is an attempt to separate three things that are currently mashed together in every article on this topic: what has actually been announced, what is being asserted by companies selling software, and what a contractor should reasonably do in November 2026.

*If a number in this space does not come with a method, it came from someone's marketing department.*

**Google's Universal Commerce Protocol launched on January 11, 2026**, as an open standard developed with retailers including Home Depot and Lowe's. **At I/O in May 2026, Google described its AI contacting local businesses on a user's behalf to book appointments**, across home repair, beauty, and pet care, with a US rollout planned for summer 2026. And as a matter of how the category works at all, agentic systems depend on products and services being presented in **structured, machine-readable formats** in order to be discoverable by an agent.

That is the confirmed set. It is smaller than the coverage suggests.

**What is not confirmed**, despite appearing widely: specific booking-lift percentages, named adoption rates among contractor software platforms, and at least one widely repeated acquisition figure. Several contractor platforms announced agentic booking endpoints or integrations during 2026, and those announcements are real as announcements — but the performance numbers attached to them trace back to the vendors selling the capability. We are not going to repeat them, for the same reason [we refused the fake voice-search statistic](/research/voice-search-for-contractors-siri-alexa-google-assistant) last year: an unsourced number in a field this new becomes a permanent citation once somebody repeats it.

<h2 id="our-data">What our own measurements add</h2>

We can contribute one piece of real data, from the surface that matters most.

Between July and October 2026 we ran 2,871 tracked AI visibility checks against one Dallas–Fort Worth plumbing market. The relevant number is Google AI Overviews: it named the tracked business in **8.1% of September checks and 48.4% of October checks** — with the caveat, [documented at length](/research/the-directory-collapse-ai-citation-shift), that our Overviews probe was broken before September and the earlier zeros measure our instrument rather than Google's behavior.

Why that matters to the agentic question specifically: **AI Overviews is the only AI surface a buyer encounters without choosing to.** A homeowner searching for a plumber gets one whether or not they have ever opened a chatbot. If Google is building agentic booking into the same product surface where it is already answering local queries — and that is what the I/O description implies — then the distribution problem and the booking problem converge on one place.

<h2 id="what-changes">What actually changes for a contracting business</h2>

Strip away the futurism and the operational change is narrow. **The first contact about a job may arrive from software working for a customer**, and software evaluates a business differently than a person does.

A person calling your shop will tolerate a voicemail, interpret a vague service page, and call back tomorrow. An agent will not do any of those things. It will take what is machine-readable, act on what is reachable, and move to the next business when it hits a dead end. There is no benefit of the doubt in the loop.

Which makes the preparation work identical to work that is already worth doing:

**Be readable.** Services, service area, and hours in text a crawler can parse — not baked into images, not hidden behind a script. This is [Index Engine Optimization](/research/is-your-site-blocking-ai-crawlers), and it was the precondition for ordinary AI visibility before anyone used the word agentic.

**Be consistent.** An agent confirming it has the right business checks the same records an engine does when deciding whether to name you: your site, your license record, your municipal listing. [Entity inconsistency](/research/claim-your-institutional-listings-ai-visibility) does not just lower your citation rate — it gives an automated process a reason to stop.

**Be reachable by something other than a ringing phone.** This is the genuine gap at most trades businesses. If the only path to booking is a person picking up, then an agent working at 9pm on a Sunday has nothing to work with. A form that posts reliably, a published scheduling link, or a phone system that captures and responds — [the front-office layer](/research/the-front-office-is-the-first-ai-hire) — is what makes you addressable at all.

**Be specific about what you actually do.** Agents match requests to capabilities. "Full-service plumbing" matches nothing in particular. "Tankless water heater installation and replacement, Tarrant and Johnson counties" matches a request.

<h2 id="what-not-to-do">What not to do yet</h2>

Do not buy an agentic booking product because of a trend piece, including this one.

The sequence that makes sense is the boring one. First, find out whether you are currently losing bookings for reasons that have nothing to do with AI — unanswered calls, forms that fail silently, a service page nobody can parse. [Measure the leak](/research/the-missed-call-ledger) before you buy a tool for it. Most contractors who do this discover a double-digit percentage of inbound contacts going nowhere, which no agentic integration will fix and which costs more than the thing the integration addresses.

Then make yourself readable and reachable, which costs an afternoon and improves your position on every surface in this article regardless of how agentic booking actually plays out.

Then, if a measured gap remains, buy something to close it.

The contractors who will do well out of this shift are not the ones who adopted earliest. They are the ones who were already easy to find, easy to verify, and easy to book — because every one of those properties pays off whether the next caller is a homeowner, an assistant acting for a homeowner, or nobody new at all.
